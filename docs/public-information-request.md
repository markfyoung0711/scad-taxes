# Public Information Request — Smith County Appraisal District

Texas Government Code Chapter 552 (the Public Information Act). FOIA, 5 U.S.C.
§ 552, binds federal agencies only and does not reach a Texas appraisal
district, so this is written under the state act.

Send to `info@smithcad.org`, or by mail to the address below. The district
must respond promptly, and within ten business days must either produce the
records, state a date certain, or seek an Attorney General ruling
(§ 552.221, § 552.301).

---

Smith County Appraisal District
Attn: Public Information Officer / Chief Appraiser
245 SSE Loop 323
Tyler, TX 75702

**Re: Request for certified appraisal rolls, tax years 2000–2025**

To the Public Information Officer:

Under the Texas Public Information Act, Chapter 552 of the Government Code, I
request copies of the following public records.

**Records requested.** The certified appraisal roll for each tax year from 2000
through 2025, in the electronic export format the District currently publishes
for tax year 2026 at `smithcad.org/data/2026/` — that is, for each year:

- Real property: the certified appraisal roll extract files (in the 2026
  release, `Extract1` and `Extract2`)
- Business personal property: the main, jurisdiction, and exemption files
- Mineral property: the main, jurisdiction, and exemption files

If the District retains these rolls in a different electronic layout for
earlier years — a fixed-width extract, a native appraisal-software export, or
any other machine-readable form — I will accept whatever format exists. I am
not asking the District to create a new record, only to produce records it
already holds. Please include any record layout or data dictionary that
accompanies them.

**Format and delivery.** Electronic delivery is preferred and should be the
least costly option: email, a download link, or an FTP/SFTP transfer. If the
volume makes that impractical, I will accept delivery on physical media and
will pay the cost of the media.

**Partial production.** If the District holds some years but not all, please
produce the years it holds rather than treating the request as unfillable, and
tell me which years are unavailable and why.

**Confidential information.** I am not requesting information the Act excepts
from disclosure — for example addresses confidential under § 552.1175. Where a
record contains both public and excepted information, please redact the
excepted portion and release the remainder (§ 552.301(a)), rather than
withholding the record entirely.

**Costs.** Please provide an itemized written estimate before incurring charges
if the total will exceed $40 (§ 552.2615). Charges should follow the Attorney
General's cost rules at 1 Tex. Admin. Code § 70.3; for records already held in
electronic form, I understand the charge is generally limited to the cost of
the media and any actual programming time required. If any charge applies, I
agree to pay up to $100 without further authorization; above that, please
contact me first.

If any part of this request is unclear, please contact me rather than denying
it — I am glad to narrow the years or the file types if that reduces the
burden.

Thank you for your assistance.

Sincerely,

Mark F. Young
mark.francis.young@gmail.com

## Notes

**Why 2000.** The District's retention schedule for certified appraisal rolls
is permanent, so the records should exist for the whole span; the practical
limit is which years survive in a machine-readable export rather than on
microfilm or paper. The partial-production paragraph is there so an incomplete
holding produces the years that do exist.

**What this buys.** The 2026 bulk roll carries 144,373 real-property accounts
with land and building values split out, appraised and assessed value, and
taxable value, rate and tax for up to 27 taxing units per account — richer than
the parcel pages, which reach back only to 2020 and require one HTTP request
per parcel. Twenty-six years of rolls would replace roughly 90,000 page fetches
per year of history.
